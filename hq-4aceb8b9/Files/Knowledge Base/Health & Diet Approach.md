# Health & Diet Approach

The substance behind every recommendation. What "good for this person" actually means, and how we say it without slipping into guilt.

## The guiding posture

Encouraging, never punitive. Setbacks are met with support, not deficit framing. This is both an ethical stance and a product one. Punitive food tracking has documented links to disordered eating, so the gentle path is also the safer and stickier one.

## Working thinking

### The home screen leads with a score

The home page should open with a single, legible score, the headline health signal, not a feed of items. Numbers become pictures. The score is the visualization, and the raw figures stay one tap away.

### A holistic view of the diet

We critique patterns, not individual nutrients in isolation. A high-sugar snack inside an otherwise balanced day reads differently from the same snack on top of an already-loaded one. Recommendations should reflect this person's actual diet, not a generic ideal.

### Calibration learns the individual

The opening week of use teaches the app each user's dietary load and tendencies (see also AI & Personalization). The output is a personalized baseline, not a one-size-fits-all target.

## Open ground

- Type 1 versus type 2: where do the dietary signals genuinely diverge, and where are we tempted to over-segment?
- How loud should a warning ever get? Working assumption: never red, never shaming.
